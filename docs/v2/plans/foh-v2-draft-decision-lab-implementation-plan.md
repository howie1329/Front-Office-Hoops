# Front Office Hoops v2 — Draft & Decision Lab Implementation Plan

**Status:** Implemented initial lab; board calibration pending
**Written against:** `4b08299`
**Date:** August 1, 2026
**Roadmap position:** Phase 2 calibration, final major pre-loop lab
**Depends on:** Population & Roster fixtures, player generation, Career Cohort transitions, Production & Value records, and the economy rookie-scale helper
**Primary surfaces:** `packages/domain-v2`, `packages/league-schema`, `packages/sim-v2`, `packages/calibration`, `apps/web-v2`

## Objective

Build a hybrid Draft & Decision Lab that does two things with the same typed
production modules:

1. Runs an interactive, two-round draft-day scenario with user and AI picks.
2. Runs seeded batches across many draft classes to measure scouting accuracy,
   board quality, draft outcomes, and three-to-five-year player results.

The lab must preserve the distinction between true player facts, public
information, team-specific scouting, private team boards, and user-authored
decisions. It must export both a full developer report and a selected-team-safe
report.

This is a calibration and decision workbench, not the authoritative League
Loop. It may use typed league-like fixtures, but it must not add draft logic to
React code, duplicate career transitions, or promote reports directly into
`LeagueDocument`.

## Product decisions locked for implementation

### Lab modes

The route exposes three related modes over shared contracts:

1. **Draft board inspector:** compare public information, a selected team’s
   private board, scouting quality, needs, mode, and rationale.
2. **Interactive draft day:** run a complete 30-team, two-round draft by
   default, with `Next pick`, `User’s next pick`, and `End draft` controls.
3. **Calibration batch:** run many classes and controlled scenarios headlessly;
   the UI configures the batch and displays/downloads the report.

Smaller scenario presets are also required for one-pick, short-draft, fixed
team, position-scarcity, and scout-tier comparison experiments.

### Draft class and order

- Standard draft class: 75 eligible prospects.
- Standard draft run: 60 selections, two rounds, 30 teams.
- Remaining eligible prospects become undrafted in the scenario result.
- Class generation reuses the existing player-generation contract and
  `draft-class` population context.
- Classes can be generated deterministically from a seed or imported as a
  validated draft-class fixture.
- Draft order is deterministic by default.
- Scenario fixtures may override order and lottery outcomes.
- Traded picks, pick protections, and full lottery behavior are out of scope
  for this lab slice.

### Information layers

Every run keeps four distinct layers:

```text
true PlayerEntity profile
        |
        +--> shared public scouting baseline
        |
        +--> team-specific scouting report
                    |
                    +--> AI team draft board
                    +--> user team draft-board workspace
```

- The public report is the same for every team.
- Each team has a manually configurable scout tier: `weak`, `average`, or
  `strong`.
- Scout quality changes range width, center accuracy, confidence, and
  categorical observability.
- Current ability and measurements are easier to estimate than potential,
  development/decline, volatility, injury resistance, and traits.
- Categorical estimates may be correct, false positive, false negative, or
  unknown.
- Reports are fixed before the draft and do not change as picks occur.
- Interviews, workouts, report refreshes, and year-round scouting are deferred,
  but the report contract must allow supplemental report types later.

### Team boards and AI decisions

Each team receives a private board built from its own scout-adjusted reports.
The baseline board score uses:

- estimated current ability;
- estimated potential/upside;
- development and volatility confidence;
- derived positional need;
- team mode;
- public mock-draft signal;
- bounded decision variance.

Team needs and modes are derived from the roster by default and may be
overridden in lab scenarios. The first modes are `contender`, `balanced`, and
`rebuilding`.

Position need is a bounded adjustment. Best available remains the dominant
signal. The public mock is a small signal or tiebreaker, not the primary
decision source. No team performs pick lookahead or predicts what the next team
will do.

At an AI pick, the highest-scoring available prospect on that team’s board is
selected. The result records the score breakdown, scouting inputs, team effects,
board rank, and strongest alternatives.

The user’s board is a workspace, not an AI policy. It is generated from the
user team’s report but permits manual rank changes, pins, notes, and “do not
draft” flags. The user may manually select any eligible available prospect.

### Public mock draft

The public mock is one shared pre-draft projection. It is fixed before the
draft and only changes by removing selected players and shifting the remaining
players. It does not receive new scouting information during the draft.

### Rookie contracts

Rookie contract assignment happens after selection:

- First-round salary comes from the pick slot’s rookie scale.
- The player’s talent does not change the salary for that slot.
- Rookie contract cost is not a prospect-ranking input.
- The assigned contract changes team payroll, cap projections, and post-draft
  team comparisons.
- Second-round contracts use a simple configurable default in this lab slice.

Reuse `EconomySnapshot.rookieScale` and `getRookieScaleSalary`; do not create a
second economy model.

### Outcome evaluation

The lab compares scouting to true facts immediately after the draft, then can
follow drafted players for three to five seasons using the existing career,
production, and value modules.

Draft quality is not measured only by immediate overall change. Reports must
also cover scouting error, realized development, production, value,
availability, position fit, draft-slot surplus, and comparison with available
alternatives.

### JSON export profiles

The lab supports two explicit profiles:

1. **Developer full:** true profiles, all public/team reports, all AI boards,
   error traces, score components, picks, contracts, outcomes, diagnostics,
   and performance data.
2. **Selected-team safe:** selected-team report and board, public mock, user
   notes/overrides, visible draft results, contracts, and safe outcomes. It
   excludes true hidden values, other teams’ private reports, and internal AI
   weights.

The full export is for calibration and replay. The safe export is for a user
perspective or future gameplay-facing inspection. Neither export is an
authoritative `LeagueDocument` save in this phase.

## Proposed contracts

Names may be refined during implementation, but the boundaries below are
required.

### Scouting contracts — `packages/domain-v2/src/draft.ts`

Add canonical types for:

```ts
type DraftScoutTier = "weak" | "average" | "strong"

type DraftScoutingCategory =
  | "measurements"
  | "current-skills"
  | "current-ability"
  | "potential"
  | "growth-curve"
  | "decline-curve"
  | "volatility"
  | "injury-resistance"
  | "role"
  | "traits"

type DraftNumericEstimate = {
  estimate: number
  range: { min: number; max: number }
  confidence: number
}

type DraftCategoricalEstimate = {
  estimate: string | null
  alternatives: string[]
  confidence: number
  status: "identified" | "uncertain" | "unknown"
}

type DraftScoutingReport = {
  version: number
  reportId: string
  prospectId: string
  source: "public" | "team"
  teamId: string | null
  scoutTier: DraftScoutTier | null
  numeric: Record<string, DraftNumericEstimate>
  categorical: Record<string, DraftCategoricalEstimate>
  roleProjection: string
  notes: string[]
}
```

The report must be safe to send to the normal UI. True values, sampled error,
and confusion diagnostics belong in a separate developer-only diagnostic type.
Use explicit category keys rather than an untyped JSON map wherever practical;
the report schema must reject unknown required structures.

### Team and board contracts

Add types for:

- `DraftTeamProfile`: team identity, roster IDs, scout tier, team mode,
  derived needs, optional manual overrides, draft assets, and initial team
  strength snapshots.
- `DraftTeamNeeds`: per-position need values plus source/reason codes.
- `DraftBoardConfig`: bounded weights for current ability, potential,
  development confidence, need, team mode, public mock, and variance.
- `DraftBoardOverride`: player ID, optional manual rank, pinned state, do-not-
  draft state, and user note.
- `DraftBoardEntry`: player ID, rank, score, score breakdown, rationale, and
  report references.
- `DraftBoard`: team ID, config version, available-player snapshot, entries,
  and generated timestamp/seed scope.

Keep `DraftTeamMode` separate from `TeamMarketStrategy`; similar labels do not
mean identical behavior.

### Draft order and result contracts

Add types for:

- `DraftOrderEntry`: round, pick, slot, team ID, and source.
- `RookieContractAssignment`: player ID, team ID, pick, round, salary years,
  mechanism, and economy snapshot reference.
- `DraftPickRecord`: pick data, selected player, board rank, score breakdown,
  alternatives, selection source (`user` or `ai`), and contract assignment.
- `DraftRunResult`: initial fixture, public mock, team profiles, reports,
  boards, picks, undrafted IDs, contracts, before/after team comparisons,
  outcome follow-up, diagnostics, and status.

### Fixture and export contracts

Add a versioned draft scenario envelope aligned with the lab strategy’s typed
fixture rules:

```ts
type DraftDecisionFixture = {
  schema: "foh-draft-decision-fixture"
  version: number
  source: {
    universeId: string
    generatorVersion: number
    configVersion: number
  }
  seed: string
  draftClass: Record<string, PlayerEntity>
  teamProfiles: Record<string, DraftTeamProfile>
  publicReports: Record<string, DraftScoutingReport>
  order: DraftOrderEntry[]
  config: DraftDecisionConfig
}
```

The full and selected-team-safe exports must have separate schema tags or an
explicit `exportProfile` discriminator. Full exports may contain truth and
diagnostics; safe exports must be validated against a schema that cannot accept
those fields accidentally.

## Implementation phases

### Phase 0 — Freeze fixture boundary and reconcile class counts

1. Decide the single source of truth for the standard draft class count.
   Implement the agreed default of 75 eligible prospects and a separate 60-pick
   draft-run setting. Preserve overrides for experiments.
2. Define the draft fixture envelope, source metadata, stable IDs, seed scopes,
   export profiles, and report versioning.
3. Add domain/schema placeholders and invalid-fixture tests before simulation.
4. Confirm that draft reports remain separate from `LeagueDocument` until the
   future league-creation/draft command exists.

Verification:

- Domain and schema typechecks pass.
- Valid 75-prospect/60-pick fixtures parse.
- 90-prospect legacy fixtures either migrate explicitly or fail with an
  actionable version/count diagnostic.
- Safe export schemas reject hidden truth and private AI-board fields.

### Phase 1 — Draft class adapter and scouting engine

1. Build a deterministic draft-class adapter that reuses the existing
   `draft-class` generation preset.
2. Add public baseline scouting with a stable seed scope independent of team
   scout tier.
3. Add team-specific scouting using `weak`, `average`, and `strong` tiers.
4. Use category-specific error configuration:
   - narrower, more accurate estimates for measurements/current skills;
   - wider, less accurate estimates for potential and trajectory;
   - categorical confusion/unknown behavior for development traits, volatility,
     injury resistance, and player traits.
5. Generate deterministic structured notes from the report. Keep user notes
   separate.
6. Add developer diagnostics containing true values, errors, confidence,
   coverage, and category-level outcome labels.
7. Ensure changing scout tier does not regenerate the underlying player.

Verification:

- Same seed and fixture produce identical public/team reports.
- Public reports are identical across teams.
- Changing only scout tier changes reports, not player truth.
- Strong scouts have narrower ranges and better center accuracy in batch
  reports; the relationship is directional, not perfect.
- Categorical reports produce bounded correct, incorrect, and unknown cases.
- No safe report or normal UI payload contains true hidden values.

### Phase 2 — Team profiles, needs, modes, and board generation

1. Create typed lab team profiles with manually configurable scout tier,
   `contender`/`balanced`/`rebuilding` mode, roster-derived needs, and scenario
   overrides.
2. Derive positional needs from roster coverage and current team strength.
   Preserve source reason codes so the UI can explain each need.
3. Add bounded board settings with a standard preset and advanced controls for
   current ability, potential, development confidence, need, mode, public mock,
   and decision variance.
4. Generate a private board from the team-specific report and current
   availability.
5. Keep best available as the dominant signal and cap need/mode/mock effects.
6. Add board rationale and top-alternative explanations.
7. Add the user-board workspace model: generated starting board, manual rank,
   pin, do-not-draft, and note overrides.

Verification:

- AI boards differ when scout tier changes while the public report stays fixed.
- Need changes affect rank within the configured cap and cannot override talent
  beyond the accepted bound.
- Modes produce directional but bounded differences.
- Public mock has a measurable small effect and cannot dominate a board.
- User overrides affect only the user board and never mutate AI boards.
- Every board entry has a stable rationale and report references.

### Phase 3 — Draft order, selection engine, and rookie contracts

1. Build deterministic 30-team/two-round/60-pick order generation.
2. Accept explicit order overrides for small scenarios and lottery fixtures.
3. Rebuild each AI team’s board after every pick by removing unavailable
   prospects; do not regenerate scouting reports.
4. Let AI teams select the highest-ranked available player on their board.
5. Let the user select any eligible available prospect at user-controlled picks,
   regardless of their board rank or team need.
6. Record pick rationale, board rank, score, alternatives, source, and remaining
   availability.
7. Assign first-round rookie-scale salaries by pick slot using the economy
   helper. Assign second-round contracts through a named simple rule.
8. Recalculate team payroll, cap position, roster strength, and positional
   coverage after each selection.
9. Keep public mock behavior to selected-player removal and projection shifts.

Verification:

- No player is selected twice.
- Every selection references an available eligible prospect and valid team/pick.
- The default run produces exactly 60 picks and a nonempty undrafted pool from
  a 75-player class.
- User selections can intentionally diverge from the user board.
- Rookie salary is determined by pick slot, not player quality.
- Team before/after snapshots reconcile roster, salary, and position counts.
- Replaying the same fixture and seed produces the same full draft.

### Phase 4 — Outcome follow-up and calibration batches

1. Add immediate scouting-versus-truth metrics:
   - numeric range coverage;
   - absolute and signed error;
   - range width by scout tier/category;
   - rank correlation;
   - categorical accuracy, false-positive, false-negative, and unknown rates.
2. Add draft-decision metrics:
   - legal selection rate;
   - duplicate/missing selection rate;
   - board-rank percentile;
   - public-mock influence;
   - need/mode effect size;
   - pick-slot surplus and alternative comparison.
3. Add 3–5-year outcome follow-up by calling existing career transition,
   production, and value modules. Do not write a second development model.
4. Report realized current ability, production, value, role, availability,
   development trajectory, and outcome versus pre-draft scouting.
5. Add benchmark profiles for 100-class directional runs and release-gate
   batches. Keep exact thresholds versioned and separate from engine config.
6. Retain failures and outliers as replayable draft fixtures.

Verification:

- Batch runs are deterministic and retain the same failed seeds.
- Strong scouts outperform weak scouts directionally without perfect
  prediction.
- AI selection remains legal across all accepted scenarios.
- Top-pick, late-first, second-round, and undrafted outcome distributions are
  reported separately.
- Outcome follow-up uses the same career/production/value functions as their
  existing labs.
- Batch reports include seed, settings, source versions, metrics, failures,
  performance, and benchmark status.

### Phase 5 — Worker boundary and JSON exports

1. Add a draft worker request/result protocol for interactive runs, batch runs,
   and optional outcome follow-up.
2. Support progress, cancellation, stale-result protection, and retained last
   completed report.
3. Serialize and validate full developer and selected-team-safe exports.
4. Add import preview for draft fixtures and full developer reports.
5. Keep safe exports unable to round-trip into hidden-truth fixtures without an
   explicit developer conversion step.

Verification:

- Long batch runs do not block the browser thread.
- Cancellation returns a truthful cancelled result and does not present a
  partial run as completed.
- Re-running an imported developer fixture reproduces the draft.
- Export/import preserves IDs, seeds, reports, picks, contracts, and outcomes.
- Safe exports contain no true profile, other-team private report, or internal
  score-weight fields.

### Phase 6 — Draft & Decision Lab route

Build the route only after the domain, simulation, schema, calibration, and
worker contracts exist.

Required panels:

- **Run setup:** generated/imported class, seed, class settings, scout tiers,
  team modes, need overrides, draft order, pick count, and board settings.
- **Public board:** public ranges, public mock, measurements, role projection,
  uncertainty, and availability.
- **Selected-team board:** team scouting report, structured notes, confidence,
  rankings, manual pins/ranks, notes, and do-not-draft controls.
- **Team comparison:** developer-only scout tier, mode, needs, board rank,
  rationale, and truth diagnostics for selected teams.
- **Draft day:** current pick, selected team, available prospects, user pick
  controls, AI rationale, top alternatives, and pick history.
- **Results:** full order, selected players, undrafted pool, rookie contracts,
  team before/after comparison, and outcome follow-up.
- **Calibration:** scout-tier comparison, error distributions, board quality,
  outcome metrics, failed seeds, and batch progress.
- **Export:** full developer and selected-team-safe JSON actions.

Required UI states:

- empty setup;
- invalid class/order/team fixture;
- loading worker;
- progress and cancellation;
- stale result after a newer run;
- no available legal selection;
- imported report with hidden diagnostics;
- completed run with benchmark pass/fail;
- safe export confirmation.

Use existing V2 shadcn-style controls and tables. Use TanStack Table for
prospect, pick-history, team-board, and outcome tables. Preserve keyboard
navigation, visible focus, responsive horizontal table scrolling, status text
that does not rely on color, and reduced-motion behavior.

### Phase 7 — Acceptance, docs, and promotion boundary

1. Run the focused domain, schema, sim, calibration, and web tests.
2. Run standard 100-class scouting and decision batches.
3. Retain representative failure fixtures.
4. Review benchmark ranges for scout tiers, board behavior, pick legality, and
   outcome distributions.
5. Update the lab strategy and V2 roadmap only after acceptance ranges pass.
6. Mark Draft & Decision as implemented/calibration pending or accepted using
   the repository’s existing status vocabulary.
7. Leave authoritative draft commands, typed league staff/team entities, and
   League Loop promotion for the later lifecycle plan.

## Calibration and invariant checklist

Hard invariants:

- Every prospect has exactly one stable ID and one draft-class membership.
- Public reports are identical across teams for the same class/seed.
- Team reports reference the correct team and scout tier.
- True player profiles are not mutated by scouting.
- Scouting reports are fixed during a draft.
- No player is selected twice.
- No pick selects an unavailable or ineligible prospect.
- Pick order and team ownership are valid.
- Rookie salary is determined by pick slot and economy snapshot.
- User board edits do not change AI board state.
- Full exports round-trip; safe exports do not contain hidden truth.
- Outcome follow-up does not mutate the original draft fixture.

Calibration targets:

- Scout quality changes range width and accuracy directionally.
- Future-facing categories are less accurate than current measurements/skills.
- Strong scouts are not perfect.
- Public mock influence remains bounded.
- Need and mode affect boards without erasing best-available behavior.
- AI choices remain understandable and legal.
- Draft-slot outcome distributions remain plausible across top, late, second,
  and undrafted groups.
- Better scouting produces better information and, directionally, better
  draft outcomes over 3–5 years.

## Explicit non-goals and stop conditions

Do not add:

- interviews, workouts, or dynamic scouting updates;
- full staff hiring/employment or a Staff Lab;
- traded picks or pick protections;
- lookahead against future teams’ picks;
- persistent multi-year organization plans;
- true talent access for AI teams;
- player-specific rookie salary negotiation;
- direct Draft commands in `LeagueDocument`;
- League Loop phase transitions;
- AI-generated narrative text.

Stop and report before improvising if:

- the 75-prospect/60-selection boundary conflicts with authoritative initial
  universe requirements;
- existing economy rules cannot represent first-round slot salaries without
  changing contract semantics;
- career/production follow-up requires a new transition model;
- safe and full export schemas cannot be separated cleanly;
- a benchmark improvement requires exposing hidden truth to normal gameplay.

## Completion criteria

The Draft & Decision Lab is complete for its first calibration slice when:

- A seeded 75-prospect class can be generated or imported.
- A full 60-pick draft can run deterministically with user and AI selections.
- Every team has a public report, team-specific report, scout tier, mode, needs,
  and private board.
- Scout tiers produce calibrated, category-specific uncertainty.
- AI boards use team information and bounded decision adjustments.
- User boards support manual ranks, pins, notes, and arbitrary legal picks.
- Public mock behavior is fixed except for selected-player removal.
- Pick-slot rookie contracts and team financial deltas are recorded.
- Immediate truth comparisons and 3–5-year outcome follow-up are available.
- Full developer and selected-team-safe JSON exports validate and round-trip.
- Batch reports retain benchmark results, failed seeds, outliers, and metrics.
- Focused tests, typechecks, and lint pass.
- The route is clearly marked calibration-only until later league integration.
